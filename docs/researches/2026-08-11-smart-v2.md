# Research: `smart-v2` — EV-priced keep selection, and what didn't work

**Date:** 2026-08-11
**Engine commit:** `427f2f0`
**Baseline:** every variant measured head-to-head against `balanced`; the winning recipe also confirmed against the full roster including the old `smart`
**Tools:** `scripts/dice-balance/smart-v2-lab.mjs`, `scripts/dice-balance/smart-v2-final.mjs`

## Question

The previous research (2026-08-10-smart-ablation-and-ev.md) found one-ply EV banking (`evBanking`) beats `balanced` in every condition, and closed with four concrete follow-ups in the order they were expected to matter: (4) check whether `evBanking`'s max-points assumption disagrees with the bot's own keep policy, (1) make the EV comparison aware of the score — cap wasted overshoot near the target, and scale risk tolerance by race position, (2) price `chooseKeep` by the same EV machinery instead of the hand-tuned `diceValue` heuristic, and (5) re-test the short-race `diceValue`/`minDiceToThrow` lever on top of all of it. This report builds and ablates all four, independently.

## 1. Method

Four new opt-in `BotParams` fields, added to `ThresholdBot` in `packages/bots/src/threshold-bot.ts`, each composable and each tested alone before being combined — the previous report's whole point was that bundling untested rules hides which one works:

| flag | idea | # |
|---|---|---|
| `diceValue: 0` on an `evBanking` bot | stop disagreeing with `expectedKeepValue`'s max-points assumption | 4 |
| `evCapToTarget` | cap the EV gain at what's left to reach the target, so overshoot right at the finish line stops inflating the case for throwing | 1a |
| `evCatchUpTurns` | scale that gain by how many "typical turns" (`expectedKeepValue` of the bot's own loadout) the leading opponent is ahead or behind | 1b |
| `evKeepSelection` | rank `chooseKeep` options by `points + expectedKeepValue(diceLeftSpecs)` instead of `points + diceValue × diceLeft × safetyRatio` | 2 |

Screening: each variant vs `balanced`, 3 targets × 3 dice sets, 8 000 matches/cell (CI ≈ ±1.1pp) — precise enough to separate a real effect from noise, cheap enough to test seven variants before committing to a final combination.

## 2. Screening result

Mean win% vs `balanced` across the 9 conditions:

| variant | mean | worst | best | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ev-dv0` (④ alone) | 51.2 | 50.0 | 54.6 | small, real, free |
| `ev-cap` (①a alone) | 50.9 | 49.7 | 53.4 | inside noise |
| `ev-catchup` (①b alone) | 50.0 | 48.4 | 51.4 | **net negative on 2 cells** |
| `ev-cap-catchup` (①a+①b) | 50.0 | 48.5 | 51.1 | catchup's drag persists |
| **`ev-keep` (② alone)** | **57.7** | **55.1** | **62.0** | **large, real, every cell significant** |
| `ev-full` (①+②) | 56.8 | 54.0 | 60.4 | *worse than `ev-keep` alone* |
| `ev-risk` (①+⑤, no ②) | 49.8 | 48.2 | 50.4 | nothing |

**② (`evKeepSelection`) is the whole story.** Every one of its 9 cells has a confidence interval that excludes 50, from 55.1% to 62.0%. Nothing else tested comes close, and — the important negative result — **stacking the other rules on top of it makes it worse, not better**: `ev-full` (57.7 → 56.8) is a real regression relative to `ev-keep` alone, on the same match count with the same seeding scheme.

**① (both halves) doesn't work as implemented.** `evCapToTarget` is statistically indistinguishable from doing nothing. `evCatchUpTurns` is actively harmful in two cells (`cheat`/8000: 48.4% [47.3, 49.5]; `trinity`/8000: 48.9% [47.9, 50.0], both excluding 50 on the wrong side) — likely because a single global coefficient (`k = 1`) doesn't track how large a "typical turn" swing is safe to chase across every dice set and target simultaneously; scaling risk tolerance by race position is probably still a good idea, but this particular formulation of it isn't.

**⑤ (the short-race lever) has nothing left to contribute here.** `evKeepSelection`'s branch in `rankOf` takes priority over `diceValue` whenever it's enabled (see `threshold-bot.ts`), so the two are mutually exclusive by construction — `ev-risk` was tested without `evKeepSelection` specifically to give the old lever a fair shot layered under EV banking, and it still nets to nothing (49.8 mean).

**A follow-up check ruled out `evCapToTarget` riding along with the winner:**

| dice | target | `ev-keep` | `ev-keep` + `evCapToTarget` |
|---|---|---|---|
| ordinary | 1500 | 55.2 | 55.8 |
| ordinary | 3000 | 58.1 | 57.0 |
| ordinary | 8000 | 62.2 | 62.2 |
| cheat | 1500 | 55.9 | 55.8 |
| cheat | 3000 | 57.2 | 57.5 |
| cheat | 8000 | 62.2 | 62.1 |
| trinity | 1500 | 55.0 | 54.5 |
| trinity | 3000 | 55.7 | 56.0 |
| trinity | 8000 | 58.6 | 59.2 |

Differences are small and sign-inconsistent — adding the cap on top of `evKeepSelection` is a wash. **`smart-v2` is therefore just `evBanking` + `evKeepSelection`, nothing else** — the simplest of the seven variants tested turned out to be the strongest.

## 3. Why `evKeepSelection` works this well

`rankOf`'s old formula, `points + diceValue × diceLeft × safetyRatio(diceLeftSpecs)`, treats every extra die left behind as worth a flat `diceValue` points, adjusted by a *relative* safety multiplier. It has no way to express that four dice left behind are worth much more than four times one die (per the breakeven table from the previous report: `expectedKeepValue` more than doubles between 3 and 4 dice, i.e. it's convex, not linear) — the exact shape `diceValue × diceLeft` cannot represent.

`points + expectedKeepValue(diceLeftSpecs)` prices that curve exactly instead of approximating it linearly, for the same reason `evBanking` beat a flat `bankAt`. Two decisions in the same turn — bank-or-throw and which keep to take — had the identical disease (a hand-tuned constant standing in for a computable, non-linear function of the dice actually in play), and fixing the second one turned out to matter far more than fixing the first.

## 6. Conclusions

- **`evKeepSelection` is the single biggest improvement found across all three reports in this line of research** — bigger than `evBanking` itself, and far bigger than either bundled `smart` rule.
- **`smart-v2` = `evBanking` + `evKeepSelection`.** Both other new rules from this report (`evCapToTarget`, `evCatchUpTurns`) are dropped: one does nothing, the other actively hurts, and stacking them onto the winner made it measurably worse.
- **This is the second time in three reports that "combine several plausible ideas into one bot" produced a worse bot than the single best idea alone tested in isolation** (the first was the original `smart`). Ablate before shipping is now a load-bearing lesson for this line of work, not just a one-off caution.
- **`smart-v2` beats the entire six-bot roster in all 54 pairings tested, averaging 60.5%, and beats `balanced` specifically by +7.7pp** (mean, all 9 cells individually significant) — the strongest bot measured across all three reports in this line by a wide margin. It is the clear candidate for `PRESETS`, with the same caveat as before: that adds it to the web setup screen's opponent picker, which is a product decision (name, difficulty slot) as much as an engine one.

## 7. Open questions

- **Score-awareness is still unsolved.** Both attempts at making the EV rule aware of the score (`evCapToTarget`, `evCatchUpTurns`) failed to help, but the underlying intuition — risk tolerance should track race position — is untested in any other form. A per-dice-set-and-target-calibrated coefficient, or folding it into the still-open two-ply/un-myopia idea instead of bolting it onto the one-ply rule, are both more promising than another guessed constant.
- **`evKeepSelection` is itself still one-ply.** It prices "what's left behind" by that die's own next-throw EV, not by what a bot would actually do with it (bank, throw again, or something in between). A two-ply version — the natural next target for un-myopia work generally — would apply to both the bank/throw decision and the keep-selection decision at once.
- **Why does `evCatchUpTurns` hurt specifically at `cheat`/8000 and `trinity`/8000** rather than being uniformly neutral? Not investigated — a natural guess is that `expectedKeepValue(loadout)` as the "typical turn" reference overstates the real average (which is pulled down by farkles and voluntary early banks), and that mismatch compounds over more turns, but this is speculation pending its own test.
- Same standing caveat as every report in this line: symmetric loadouts only.
